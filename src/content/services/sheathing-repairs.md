Not every framing job is a whole house. Some of the most important work is the finishing detail that holds a project together: sheathing, blocking and backing, shear wall nailing and corrections that clear an inspection.

Sheathing ties the frame together and makes it rigid. On shear walls, the panel layout and nailing schedule set by your engineer are what the inspector checks, so we follow them exactly. On roofs, sheathing gets the building ready to be dried in. We sheathe new frames and replace damaged sheathing on existing ones.

Blocking and backing are the small pieces that save headaches later. Solid wood between studs and joists gives cabinets, vanities, handrails, grab bars, shelving and wall-mounted TVs something solid to fasten to. Adding blocking before drywall takes minutes. Adding it after takes a hole in a finished wall.

If your framing inspection came back with notes, we can help clear them. We look at the inspector's list and the drawings, explain what's needed and do the corrections so your project can move on to the next stage. We also finish or correct framing another crew started.

For homeowners, we repair framing damaged by rot, water, insects or past renovations. We open up the area, find out how far the damage goes, and replace or reinforce what's needed. Where the structure is affected, we work to an engineer's direction.

We also reframe for plan changes, such as moving a doorway, adding a window or adjusting a wall after the layout changes. Small jobs are welcome. We may schedule them around larger projects, but we'll always tell you when we can be there.
