The roof is where framing mistakes show the most. A ridge that sags, a valley that doesn't line up or a truss set out of plumb will show up in the roofing, the ceilings and sometimes in leaks. We take roof framing seriously because it's the part of the house that protects everything else.

Most new homes in the Lower Mainland use engineered roof trusses. We plan the delivery and lift with you or your GC, set the trusses to the layout, and install the temporary and permanent bracing shown on the truss drawings. Girder trusses, hangers and point loads get the attention the engineer calls for.

Not every roof is trusses. Vaulted ceilings, lofts, dormers, additions and complex rooflines often need stick framing: rafters, ridge beams, hips and valleys cut and fitted on site. We're comfortable with both, and on many houses we combine them.

Before we start, we review the architectural, structural and truss drawings together, because roof problems often hide where they meet. If a bearing point doesn't line up or a beam looks undersized for the load, we raise it before the crane arrives.

Once the structure is up, we frame overhangs and fascia backing, add blocking where the roofers and other trades need it, and sheathe the roof so it's ready to be dried in. In our wet climate, getting a roof closed in quickly matters, so we plan our sequence around the forecast.

We also reframe existing roofs for additions, new dormers and repairs. Wherever the structure changes, we work to engineered drawings so the new roof is as solid as the rest of the house.
