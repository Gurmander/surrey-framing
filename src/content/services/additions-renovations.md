Framing an addition is part carpentry and part detective work. New homes start from a level slab and fresh drawings. Older homes have settled, been renovated before and hidden a few surprises behind the drywall. The new framing has to tie into all of that cleanly, so floors line up, rooflines meet and the house works as one building.

We start by looking closely at the existing structure. Where does the load come down? Is the floor level? What's actually inside the wall you want to open up? We compare what we find to your structural drawings and flag anything that doesn't match before work starts.

For rear and side additions, we frame floors, walls and roofs that tie into the original house. For second storeys, we frame new floor and wall systems on top of the existing structure, working to the engineer's design for beams, posts and point loads. Inside the house, we remove load-bearing walls with temporary shoring, set new beams and posts, and frame new window and door openings.

Renovation work happens in someone's home, and we act like it. We protect floors and finishes where we're working, keep dust and debris contained as much as we can, and clean up at the end of each day. If you're living in the house during the work, we'll plan access and noisy days with you.

We also work with renovation contractors who need a framing crew for the structural part of a job. We show up when scheduled, follow the drawings and leave the work ready for inspection and the trades that follow.

Whatever the size of the job, the goal is the same: an addition that feels like it was always part of the house.
